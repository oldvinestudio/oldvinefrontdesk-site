How to file a fictitious business name (DBA) in Riverside County

A fictitious business name statement, often called a DBA ("doing business as"), is how Riverside County records who is behind a business name. Here's who needs one, what the county charges, and the publishing step that's easy to miss. Fees as listed by each agency on November 19, 2026.

On this page

  3. How to file, step by step
  4. Deadlines to put on your calendar

Do you need one?

Riverside County's fictitious business name page says a name counts as fictitious when:

  • You're an individual and the business name doesn't include your surname, or it suggests additional owners, such as "Company" or "Brothers".
  • You're a partnership and the name doesn't include every general partner's surname, or it suggests additional owners.
  • You're a corporation using a name that isn't the one in its articles of incorporation.

Nonprofits such as churches and labor unions don't file. When a corporation, LLC or limited partnership files, the county asks for a Certificate of Status from the California Secretary of State. Riverside County fictitious business names

Two examples

  • "Maria Lopez Cleaning", run by Maria Lopez on her own, includes her surname and suggests no other owners, so it generally isn't fictitious.
  • "Valley Fresh Cleaning", run by the same person, is a fictitious name, so she files a statement.

What it costs

Statement with one business name and one registrant (includes one certified copy)$66
Each additional business name at the same address, or each additional registrant$5
Extra certified copy$2
Statement of abandonment (when you stop using the name)$31
Newspaper publicationSet by the newspaper you choose; ask it for a price

The county doesn't refund filing fees, and you can't change a statement after you file it without filing a new one and paying again. Check every line before you submit.

How to file, step by step

  1. Search the name. Search the county's online registry to see whether the name is already on file.
  2. Fill in the online application. You get an order number (the SST number). Keep it.
  3. File and pay. Pay online, or print and sign the form and take it to the Clerk's office (an appointment is recommended), mail it with a check or money order payable to Riverside County Clerk to P.O. Box 751, Riverside, CA 92502-0751, or leave it in a county drop box. Some adjudicated newspapers in the county will also file it for you.
  4. Publish the notice. Publish the statement in an adjudicated newspaper once a week for four consecutive weeks, starting within 45 days of filing. The county keeps a list of approved newspapers.
  5. File proof of publication. File the newspaper's affidavit of publication with the County Clerk within 45 days after publication ends.

Deadlines to put on your calendar

  • File within 40 days of first doing business under the name.
  • A statement lasts 5 years. Renew before it expires. A renewal filed within 40 days after expiration with no changes doesn't need to be republished.
  • Moving? A new business location means a new statement.

What a DBA doesn't do

A DBA is a public record of who is behind a business name. It isn't a trademark registration, it doesn't create a separate company, and it doesn't stop someone with earlier rights to a similar name from objecting. Before you print cards and signs, run basic searches:

  • USPTO trademark search
  • California Secretary of State business search
  • The domain name and the social handles you want

Questions

Common questions

Something missing? Email a question.

How much does a DBA cost in Riverside County?

The county lists $66 for a statement with one business name and one registrant, plus $5 for each additional name at the same address or each additional registrant. Newspaper publication is extra, at the newspaper's price.

Can I file a fictitious business name online in Riverside County?

Yes. Every filing starts with the county's online application, and you can pay online. You can also take the printed form in person, mail it or use a county drop box.

How long does a Riverside County DBA last?

Five years. Renew it before it expires; a renewal within 40 days after expiration with no changes doesn't need to be republished.

Do I really have to publish it in a newspaper?

Yes. The county requires the statement to run once a week for four consecutive weeks in an adjudicated newspaper, starting within 45 days of filing, and then the affidavit of publication must be filed within 45 days after it ends.

Does a DBA protect my business name?

No. It's a public record of who is behind the name, not a trademark. Run a trademark and business name search first, and ask an attorney about trademark protection.
