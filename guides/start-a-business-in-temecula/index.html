How to start a business in Temecula: licenses, permits and costs

Step 1: Choose your business structure

Sole proprietor, partnership, LLC or corporation. The choice affects your taxes and personal liability, so talk it through with a CPA or an attorney, or with a free adviser at the Small Business Development Center or SCORE (step 8). Sole proprietors skip step 2.

Step 2: Register an LLC or corporation with the state

LLCs and corporations file with the California Secretary of State through bizfile Online, which also shows the current state filing fees.

Step 3: Get an EIN if you need one

Corporations, partnerships, LLCs with more than one member and anyone with employees generally need an employer identification number. Apply directly with the IRS, which doesn't charge for it. IRS: Get an employer identification number

Step 4: File a fictitious business name if you trade under one

If your business name isn't your own legal name (or your company's registered name), file a fictitious business name statement with Riverside County. The county lists $66 for the first name and owner, and you then publish the statement in a newspaper once a week for four weeks. File within 40 days of first doing business. Every step is in the Riverside County DBA guide.

Step 5: Get your City of Temecula business license

Anyone doing business within Temecula city limits needs a City of Temecula business license (Temecula Municipal Code section 5.04.030). The city's instructions page has the forms. Temecula business license instructions

  • How to apply. Online is the city's preferred method, through its online application. You can also apply by mail with a check payable to the City of Temecula. Don't email card details; the city sends an invoice to pay online.
  • What to include. Fill in every section of the application packet, sign and date it, and describe what you'll do on the Statement of Operations form. If someone files for you, they need an agent letter of authorization from you.
  • Special cases. Massage and tobacco applications are filed in person at City Hall, 41000 Main Street. Door-to-door solicitors need police sign-off on the application.

Fees the city lists (the registration fee is non-refundable):

License typeFeeWhat it includes

Every license expires January 31, and renewing is up to you. The city adds a $15 late fee after January 31 and a $35 late fee after March 1.

Step 6: Check zoning and occupancy for your location

  • Commercial space. Before you sign a lease, check with the city's Planning (zoning), Building and Fire teams. The city requires a Certificate of Occupancy for new businesses, relocations and ownership or name changes that involve a change of tenancy without tenant improvements. Any construction or tenant improvements go through Building and Safety. Temecula Certificate of Occupancy
  • Working from home. File the city's Home Occupation form with your license application. It carries the one-time $20 fee included in the $59 above. Home Occupation form (PDF)

Step 7: Get the state and county permits your trade needs

The city asks you to contact any required agencies before you apply. Common ones:

  • Selling goods: a seller's permit from the California Department of Tax and Fee Administration. CDTFA: seller's permits
  • Construction and home improvement work: a state contractor's license where the job requires one. Contractors State License Board
  • Food and alcohol: plan approval from the Riverside County Department of Environmental Health for food facilities, and a license from the state Department of Alcoholic Beverage Control for alcohol. Riverside County Environmental Health: plan check · California ABC: apply for a license
  • Not sure? CalGold lists permits by city and business type. CalGold

Step 8: Use the free local help

  • Entrepreneurial Resource Center (ERC) Temecula, 43200 Business Park Drive, 2nd floor. Led by CSUSB with the City of Temecula and Riverside County, it hosts the Inland Empire Small Business Development Center and the Inland Empire Women's Business Center, which offer business counseling, training and workshops. ERC Temecula
  • TVE2 (Temecula Valley Entrepreneurs Exchange), the city's program with no-cost consulting, workshops and an incubator for qualified startups. TVE2
  • SCORE Inland Empire, free mentoring and workshops. SCORE Inland Empire

Step 9: Get found once you're registered

Customers look you up before they call. Google lists a business on Maps only if it meets customers in person, at its location or across a service area, so read Google's Business Profile guidelines before you set one up. After that: a name and logo that read well on a phone, a simple website, and cards or a sign if customers see you in person.

Questions

Common questions

Something missing? Email a question.

How much does a business license cost in Temecula?

The City of Temecula lists $39 for a commercial or industrial business license ($35 registration plus a $4 state fee) and $59 for a home-based business, which includes a one-time $20 home occupation fee. Nonprofits and veterans pay $4, or $24 if home-based. Check the city's page for current fees.

Do I need a business license to work from home in Temecula?

Yes. Anyone doing business inside city limits needs one, and home-based businesses also file the Home Occupation form with the application.

When does a Temecula business license expire?

Every January 31. The city adds a $15 late fee after January 31 and a $35 late fee after March 1.

Do I file a DBA with the City of Temecula?

No. Fictitious business names are filed with the Riverside County Assessor-County Clerk-Recorder, not the city. See the Riverside County DBA guide.

Is there free help for starting a business in Temecula?

Yes. The Entrepreneurial Resource Center on Business Park Drive hosts the Small Business Development Center and the Women's Business Center, the city runs TVE2, and SCORE Inland Empire offers free mentoring.
