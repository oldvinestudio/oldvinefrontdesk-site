Temecula Valley launch checklist

The paperwork side of opening a business in the Temecula Valley, in a sensible order, with a link to the official source for each step. It's free, there's nothing to sign up for, and you can print it.
Email me the link

  1. Choose your business structure

    Sole proprietor, partnership, LLC or corporation. The choice affects taxes and personal liability, so talk it through with a CPA or an attorney, or with a free SBDC or SCORE adviser.

  2. Register an LLC or corporation with the state

    LLCs and corporations file with the California Secretary of State; sole proprietors skip this step. California Secretary of State bizfile Online

  3. Get an EIN if you need one

    Corporations, partnerships, LLCs with more than one member and anyone with employees generally need an employer identification number. Apply directly with the IRS. IRS: Get an employer identification number

  4. File a fictitious business name if you use one

    Trading under a name that isn't your own legal name (or your company's registered name) means filing a fictitious business name statement with the Riverside County Assessor-County Clerk-Recorder, then publish it in a newspaper for four weeks. Step by step: how to file a DBA in Riverside County. Riverside County fictitious business names

  5. Get your city business license or registration

    Every city in the valley has its own:

    City business license or registration
    TemeculaCity of Temecula business license; home-based businesses also file the Home Occupation form. Temecula business license instructions · Full Temecula guide
    MurrietaCity of Murrieta business license, applied for online; home-based businesses check the home occupation permit form. Murrieta business licenses
    MenifeeCity of Menifee business license. Menifee business license FAQs
    Lake ElsinoreCity of Lake Elsinore business license; home-based applications go to the Planning Counter for approval. Lake Elsinore business licenses
    WildomarCity of Wildomar business registration, online. Wildomar business registration

  6. Get a seller's permit if you sell goods

    If you sell or lease physical goods in California, you generally need a seller's permit from the California Department of Tax and Fee Administration. CDTFA: Obtaining a seller's permit

  7. Check for state licenses and permits

    Some work needs a state license (contractors, for example). CalGold lists permits by city and business type. CalGold

  8. Set up your Google Business Profile

    Google allows a profile only for a business that meets customers in person, at its location or across a service area. Google's guidelines for Business Profiles

  9. Use the free local help

    Temecula's TVE2 and Entrepreneurial Resource Center, the Small Business Development Center and SCORE Inland Empire all offer no-cost advice. TVE2 · Entrepreneurial Resource Center · SCORE Inland Empire
